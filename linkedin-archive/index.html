Read this before December

Download your LinkedIn data before it's gone. Then put it to work.

Right now you can download a file from LinkedIn that lists every person you are connected to, with the company they work at and the job they hold. In December that file gets thinner. The companies and job titles come out, and once they are out, you cannot get them back.

What LinkedIn is taking out

Names, profile links, and the dates you connected stay in the download. Email addresses, current companies, and job titles come out.

LinkedIn has not announced the exact day in December, so work to November 30 and you are safe.

Why it matters: the company and title columns turn a list of names into a map of where your people actually work today.

Get the full file, two minutes

Do this on a computer, signed in to LinkedIn:

  1. Click your profile photo, then Settings & Privacy.
  2. Choose Data privacy, then Download your data.
  3. Pick the larger archive, the one with everything in it.
  4. Click Request archive.
LinkedIn needs up to a day to build it, then emails you a link. The link dies after 72 hours. When the email lands, download the file and keep it somewhere safe.

The file holds more than names

The connections list is the part with the deadline. The rest of the archive is your whole working life on the platform, in plain spreadsheets:

  1. Every post you have ever published, full text, with dates.
  2. Every comment you have written and every reaction you have handed out.
  3. Your messages, the whole inbox, sent and received.
  4. Endorsements and recommendations, the ones you gave and the ones people wrote about you.
  5. Your jobs, schools, skills, and certifications, in order, with dates.
  6. Who you follow, and the file where LinkedIn lists what it thinks you care about, the guesses it sells to advertisers.

The connections are why you grab it this weekend. The rest is why you keep it for good.
