A heads up, and an offer

Download your LinkedIn data before December. Then put it to work.

In December, LinkedIn cuts its data download. The file you can pull of your own connections stops including their current companies and job titles. That is the most useful part of the file, and it is about to go away.

What is changing

LinkedIn is changing what you can download about your own network. Names, profile links, and connection dates stay in. Companies, job titles, and email addresses come out.

LinkedIn has not named the exact day, so treat November 30 as the deadline. The company and title columns are the prize: together they are a dated snapshot of where everyone you know works right now.

Grab yours, about two minutes

On a computer, signed in to LinkedIn:

  1. Click your photo, then Settings & Privacy.
  2. Choose Data privacy, then Download your data.
  3. Pick the larger archive, not just the basic file.
  4. Click Request archive.
LinkedIn takes up to a day to prepare the archive, then emails you a download link. The link lives for 72 hours, so save the file when the email lands.

It is not just your connections

The larger archive holds your whole LinkedIn life, in plain files:

  1. Every post you have published, with the full text and the date.
  2. Every comment you have written and every reaction you have given.
  3. Your full message history, in and out, with names and dates.
  4. Endorsements and recommendations, given and received. Other people, telling your story.
  5. Your profile history: jobs, education, skills, certifications. Your progress, with dates on it.
  6. Who and what you follow, plus the file where LinkedIn writes down what it thinks you are interested in, so it can sell ads against you.

Your connections carry the deadline. The rest is the reason to keep the file forever.
