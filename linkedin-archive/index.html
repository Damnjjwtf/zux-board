Read this before December

Download your LinkedIn data before it's gone. Then put it to work.

Right now, LinkedIn lets you download a file listing every person you are connected to, with their company and job title. In December, the companies and titles come out. Once they are out, you cannot get them back.

What LinkedIn is taking out

Names, profile links, and connection dates stay. Email addresses, current companies, and job titles go.

LinkedIn has not named the exact day. Work to November 30 and you are safe.

The company and title columns are what turn a list of names into a map of where your people work today.

Get the full file, two minutes

On a computer, signed in to LinkedIn:

  1. Click your profile photo, then Settings & Privacy.
  2. Choose Data privacy, then Download your data.
  3. Pick the larger archive, the one with everything in it.
  4. Click Request archive.
LinkedIn takes up to a day to build it, then emails a link that lives for 72 hours. Download the file when the email lands, and keep it somewhere safe.

The file holds more than names

The connections carry the deadline. The rest of the archive is your working life on the platform, in plain spreadsheets:

  1. Every post you have published, full text, with dates.
  2. Every comment you have written, every reaction you have given.
  3. Your full message history, sent and received.
  4. Endorsements and recommendations, given and received.
  5. Your jobs, education, skills, and certifications, with dates.
  6. Who you follow, and what LinkedIn thinks you are interested in, the guesses it sells to advertisers.

Grab it for the connections. Keep it for everything else.
